## At a glance

Custom software development creates an application around business needs, such as request management, a portal, case tracking or an internal system. Bump analyses the required operation, designs the interface with users and develops the features and integrations needed to connect existing tools.

## Buy, integrate or build?

Custom software is not automatically the best answer. An existing product may fit if its features cover your needs and its operation remains acceptable. An integration may be enough when the problem sits between two tools. Custom development becomes relevant when your process requires features poorly covered by existing solutions.

| Option | Consider it when… |
| --- | --- |
| Existing product | An available solution covers most needs |
| Integration | The tools fit but information does not move well between them |
| Custom software | The business need justifies a specific interface or logic |

## Bring work together in the right place.

A business application can make stages, owners and decision-relevant information visible. The aim is to reduce workarounds and repeated entry, not multiply screens.

- Web applications designed for daily tasks.
- Portals that centralize requests and documents.
- Internal tracking and coordination systems.
- Dashboards and reports connected to useful data.
- CRM, ERP and API connections.

## Build around a clear scope.

We translate the business need into features, journeys and acceptance criteria. The people who will use the system contribute to its design. Access controls, data migration, dependencies and maintenance are considered from the start.

Implementation follows the agreed scope. Testing should cover everyday cases and exceptions: denied access, missing data, an unavailable service or an action that needs recovery.

## Plan for life after launch.

Software depends on hosting, libraries, access and sometimes other services. The proposal should specify deliverables, documentation, maintenance conditions and responsibilities. Code handover, operation and ownership terms are defined in the engagement.

Future changes are prioritized around actual use. A coherent initial scope lets the team evaluate operation before adding capabilities.

## What should you validate before launching custom software?

Launch preparation includes the people who will use the software. Bump defines the workflows to test and the conditions for real use within the agreed engagement. The software must address the selected need, and your team must know how to operate it.

- Use: complete agreed tasks with representative cases.
- Access: check roles and the information available to each person.
- Connections: test data exchanges and errors in connected systems.
- Recovery: explain how to correct data or return to a known working state.
- Handover: provide documentation and agree on support after launch.

## Frequently asked questions

### What kinds of software can Bump develop?

Our services include web applications, portals, internal systems, dashboards and integrations. Feasibility and scope are confirmed after analysing your needs.

### Can the software connect to our existing systems?

Yes. CRM, ERP and API integrations are part of our offer. Available features, permissions, limitations and potential service costs need to be assessed.

### How long does development take?

The timeline depends on scope, integrations, data migration and approvals. We specify a schedule in the proposal after assessing these factors; the site does not state a universal delivery time.

### Is maintenance included?

Maintenance is a stage in our model, but its scope and terms are defined in the proposal. Corrections, updates, operations and new features need to be distinguished.

### Who maintains the software after launch?

The proposal should define responsibility for hosting, access, backups, fixes and changes. Bump agrees on the maintenance scope with your team. Launch and ongoing support both need to be defined; no level of availability should be assumed without agreement.
